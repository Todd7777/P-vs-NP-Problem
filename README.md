# P vs NP: an honest attack dossier (November 2026)

**Status of the problem: open.** This repository does not contain a proof that P = NP or P ≠ NP, and
it does not claim one. It contains the output of one sustained, resourced attempt to attack the
problem with everything available in a single session: the exact theorems that constrain any
proof (with proofs), the state of the art on every live front as of 5 November 2026 (compiled by
six parallel literature surveys with verification tags), a log of nine concrete attack attempts
pushed until each one died at a precisely named obstruction, a ranked research program, a list of
24 open sub-problems ordered by leverage, and reproducible computational experiments.

If you are looking for the one-paragraph answer: see `docs/00-executive-summary.md`.

## The short version

1. Any proof of P ≠ NP must be non-relativizing, non-algebrizing, and (if it goes through circuit
   lower bounds) non-natural and non-local. These are theorems (`docs/02-barriers.md`).
2. Exactly one family of techniques is known to evade the classical barriers — Williams' algorithmic
   method and its descendants (range avoidance, iterative win-win) — and it currently reaches
   NQP ⊄ ACC0 and Σ2E ⊄ SIZE(2^n/n). Every known route from there down to NP needs an argument that
   works at polynomial scale without a spare quantifier or an exponential time budget; none exists.
3. The 2026 frontier is an explicit-construction problem (Range Avoidance in FP^NP), a non-local
   lower-bound technique (hardness magnification), NP-hardness of meta-complexity problems, and
   AC0[p]-Frege lower bounds. None of these resolves P vs NP alone; each is where a new idea would
   first register.
4. The reported October 2026 AI-generated, Lean-checked refutations of the Exponential Time
   Hypothesis are under expert review and, by their authors' own statement, have no bearing on P vs NP.

## Experiments

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
pytest                                         # ~15 s; RUN_SLOW=1 pytest adds ~30 s of slower checks
cd experiments
python3 exact_circuit_complexity.py n4         # first encoding: all 222 NPN classes of 4-input functions vs Knuth
python3 exact_circuit_complexity.py explicit   # first encoding: explicit 4/5-input functions (intervals if the budget runs out)
python3 validate_encoding.py all               # stronger encoding vs brute force (n=3), Knuth (n=4, B2), first encoding (n=4, U2)
python3 certify_costs.py run --proofs DIR      # exact costs with every lower bound as a DRAT proof checked by drat-trim
python3 certify_costs.py summarize && python3 build_final_table.py
python3 compare_optimal5.py PATH/knuthies.dat  # compare with Knuth's optimal chains for all 5-input functions (optimal5)
python3 proof_complexity_php.py                # CDCL on pigeonhole (exponential) vs ordering principle (polynomial)
```

drat-trim is not a Python package: build it from https://github.com/marijnheule/drat-trim with
`make`, then put it on `PATH` or set `DRAT_TRIM`. Tests that need it are skipped without it.

the rest, with every smaller gate count refuted by a drat-trim-checked proof when the label is

The lower bounds of 13 for MOD3_5 and EXACTLY2_5 add gate elimination to the SAT encoding.
Setting an input to a constant deletes every gate that reads it, and every reader of a gate that the constant makes
constant. MOD3_5's restrictions MOD3_4 and EXACTLY2_4 cost 10 and 9 in U2 (Machine-checked,
`experiments/results/restriction_costs.json`), so in a 12-gate circuit for MOD3_5 every input has
fan-out at most 2, with similar bounds around each gate that reads an input
(`experiments/ENCODING.md` §5 proves that adding them keeps a minimum circuit). With these
constraints the 12-gate instance, split into 115 cubes, is refuted in 10 CPU-minutes, every proof
checked by drat-trim (13 CPU-minutes, 1.1 GB of proofs, not committed); without them 41 of the
same cubes were still undecided after 6 million conflicts each. EXACTLY2_5's restrictions EXACTLY2_4
and EXACTLY1_4 both cost 9, which bounds fan-out only by 3; with the stronger union form of the
constraint (fixing x_v = c also deletes the readers of every gate that c makes constant) the same
115 cubes are refuted in 2.5 CPU-hours, checked by drat-trim in 4.7 (9.7 GB of proofs, each
deleted after its check; the hashes are recorded).

The upper bounds of the two open entries are verified circuits. EXACTLY2_5's 14 comes from
`experiments/local_improve.py`, which re-synthesizes small windows of a circuit with SAT (local
improvement after Kulikov, Pechenev and Slezkin, with the relaxation that a replacement only has to
agree on the window-input combinations that occur): in the 15-gate circuit found by Kissat, the gates
x1 ∨ x3 and (x2 ∨ x3) ∨ (x1 ∨ x3) collapse into x1 ∨ (x2 ∨ x3). MOD3_5's 15 is the XOR expansion of a
12-gate B2 circuit with two XORs, one dead gate pruned. Local improvement finds nothing further in either circuit over
every window of up to 10 gates (at most 11 inputs, 200,000 conflicts per SAT call).

### Symmetric functions of 5 inputs in U2

A symmetric function of 5 inputs is fixed by its values b_0..b_5 on inputs of weight 0..5. Negating
the output or every input does not change the cost, so the 62 nonconstant ones form 19 classes,
listed here by their smallest vector with b_0 = 0 (`experiments/symmetric5_u2.py`,
`experiments/results/symmetric5_u2.json`). Every lower bound starts from Lemma 5(a) of
`experiments/ENCODING.md` (C(f) ≥ C(f|x_v=c) + 1, with all 16 restriction costs Machine-checked in
`results/restriction_costs.json`) and is raised k by k with the gate-elimination constraints, each k
refuted by a proof that drat-trim checked (`certify_u2_suite.py --elim 3`). Upper bounds are verified
circuits, mostly from local improvement of XOR-expanded B2 circuits, and several are probably not
optimal. The B2 column is Knuth's (optimal5); since every U2 circuit is a B2 circuit it is also a lower
bound for U2, which our own lower bounds already reach in every row.

The same method completes the U2 table for 4 variables: the four NPN classes that the earlier runs
left open all have U2 cost 10, so over all 65,536 functions of 4 variables the U2 cost is
0, 1, ..., 10 for 10, 48, 256, 940, 2,336, 6,464, 10,616, 18,984, 17,680, 7,882 and 320 functions

The DRAT proofs (about 2 GB) are not in the repository. CaDiCaL is deterministic, so
`python3 certify_costs.py recheck --proofs DIR` regenerates every proof bit for bit (the SHA-256 of
each CNF and proof is in `results/certificates.json`) and runs drat-trim on it again.

## Provenance and caveats

- Written in one session on 5 November 2026 by Claude (Anthropic), with six parallel research
  sub-agents for the literature. The session's network policy blocked direct fetches of arXiv, ECCC
  and ACM DL; citations were verified through search-engine extracts of those pages and through
  GitHub. Items that could not be verified were marked **[U]**; on 10 Nov 2026 each of them was
  checked at its source (arXiv, ECCC, Dagstuhl LIPIcs, STOC lists) and corrected where needed, see the
  verification log in `docs/bibliography.md`. Results after June 2026 are marked ★.
- All mathematics in `docs/02` and `docs/05` is standard; the proofs were written from scratch and
  should be checked like any other proof. `docs/06` contains one deliberately preserved false
  derivation with its error identified (§6.3).
- Nothing here is peer reviewed.
